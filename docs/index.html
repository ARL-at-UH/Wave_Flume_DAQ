Wave Flume DAQ — Sensor Interfaces, Cabling & Panel Layout

Eight analog sensors (4 wave-height gauges, 3 load cells, 1 differential pressure transducer) into one MCC USB-1608G — 16-bit, all eight channels differential — with signal conditioning, power distribution, and DIN-rail terminal wiring on a single mounting panel. Pinouts below are taken from the manufacturer manuals cited at the end.

Read first

Three things the manuals changed

Power — supply settings must change

The FUTEK IAA100 amplifiers require 12–30 VDC (current spec drawing FI1573; 1 W max each, but 1.2 A inrush — hence a time-delay fuse on the 24 V feed). The Setra 239 requires 22–30 VDC (label-confirmed) excitation. The panel is now self-powered: one mains inlet feeds three Class II DIN-rail supplies (all Mean Well HDR family, floating outputs): A2 HDR-15-24 for the 24 V amplifier rail (GND_24), A4 HDR-15-12 for the gauges' floating 12 V rail (GND_12 — gauges accept 8–24 V), and A3 HDR-15-24 as the Setra's isolated island (GND_24ISO). The BK 1672 retires to the bench for teaching setups and current-limited commissioning.

Power — the pressure transducer needs an isolated 24 V source

The Setra 239 is a true 4-terminal device: its manual states the −Excitation lead must never be connected to the −Output lead. −Output carries the measurement reference: it feeds the DAQ's CH7L input and makes the island's single tie to analog ground — and the 24 V amplifier rail is also referenced to analog ground (via the A6 jumper, §6). Powering the Setra from that shared 24 V rail would tie −Exc to −Out through ground. Fix: feed the Setra from its own floating source — a dedicated Class II DIN-rail supply (A3, Mean Well HDR-15-24) whose output serves the X1–X2 pair and nothing else — its return is net GND_24ISO, the third ground domain. This matches the Setra manual's multi-hookup rules — and with the 1608G's differential inputs the setup now combines the manual's Option 1 (differential readout) and Option 2 (isolated supply).

DAQ — corrected to USB-1608G: all eight channels run differential

Hardware correction (2026-10): the lab's actual DAQ is an MCC USB-1608G, not the USB-1208FS-Plus this document originally assumed. That upgrade dissolves the old constraint: the 1608G offers 16 single-ended or 8 differential inputs (16-bit, range software-selectable per channel: ±10/±5/±2/±1 V), so all eight sensors now run differential — each channel measures its sensor's HI against its own LO terminal, with 86 dB of common-mode rejection at 60 Hz. Every channel is a twisted pair from its landing strip (as built: gauges + pressure = white HI / blue LO; load cells = yellow HI / green LO). Three rules make it work: (1) set the device to DIFF mode in InstaCal before trusting any terminal name — the board silkscreen shows single-ended names, so wire by terminal number; (2) each power island keeps exactly one DC reference to AGND (§6) so the inputs stay inside the ±10.2 V common-mode window; (3) ranges: CH0–CH6 ±10 V, CH7 ±5 V (the Setra puts out ±2.5 V). Never land a supply wire on a DAQ terminal — absolute maximum is ±25 V to AGND.

§1 · System

Interface overview

Everything lands on DIN-rail terminal blocks first — sensors are field-replaceable without rewiring the DAQ. Every sensor detaches at a panel bulkhead connector, and every position is an M12 pair — a Phoenix push-lock plug on the field wires mating a TE receptacle whose 0.5 m pigtail runs into the box: wave gauges at J1–J4 (B-code), amplified load-cell lines at J5–J7 and pressure at J8 (A-code; the IAA100s live at the flume next to their cells). B-coded J1–J4 physically cannot mate the A-coded positions; within J5–J8 the connectors are identical, so label them. Only the bench supply leads and USB enter through glands.

Every mating surface, pin by pin

IF-A — Wave gauge connector (gauge ↔ cable W1–W4)

Each AWP-24-3 has a 6-pin circular bayonet receptacle, Amphenol PT02A10-6P. The mating cable plug PT06E-10-6S (AWPC-01) ships with each gauge. Twist the white signal wire tightly with its black return the whole way: that pair replaces the shielded pair Akamina recommends, so keep the runs as short as practical.

IF-B — Panel bulkheads J1–J4 (cable ↔ panel, M12 B-code)

The panel end of each gauge cable W1–W4 gets a TE T4110402051-000 field-wireable M12 B-coded plug — unshielded plastic body, screw terminals, 18–24 AWG, PG9 gland (heat-shrink the twisted bundle where the gland clamps — it seals on a round cable, ~6–8 mm). It mates panel receptacle J1/J2/J3/J4, a TE T4171010405-002 (M12 B-code 5-pos male, rear-mount, 0.5 m pigtail) whose leads land on the DIN rails. B-coding is the keying: these four cannot be plugged into the A-coded J5–J8, and vice versa. The shield-substitute drain rides contact 5, so the shield grounds only at the GND bus (Akamina's single-point rule) — the plastic plug has no shell to terminate.

IF-C — Load cell ↔ amplifier (IAA100 sensor-side terminal, 4-pos)

The amplifiers live at the flume, next to their load cells (splash hood or small enclosure — they are not submersible). The LSB210's integral 5 ft cable (29 AWG, 4-conductor, spiral-shielded silicone) wires directly into the IAA100's 4-position sensor terminal block — the "cable entry housing, 4POS, sensor side" you already have. Fold or tin the fine 29 AWG leads so the terminals grip. Per the current FUTEK manual, the cable shield lands on the amp's CHASSIS screw (the enclosure body), not on pin 4 — add a short bond wire from the chassis screw to the output-ground terminal so the shield is referenced (one point only; the cell end floats). IF-E — Pressure transducer flying leads (Setra 239)

The 239 has a captive cable with one of two factory color codes — check yours against both columns. It plugs in at panel connector J8 — the same TE T4110001051-000 plug + TE T4171010005-002 receptacle pair as J5–J7 (contacts: 1 = +Exc, 2 = −Exc, 3 = +Out, 4 = −Out, 5 = shield/case). Because J8 is physically identical to the amp jacks J5–J7, label all four unmistakably — cross-plugging misroutes the 24 V domains (iso-module overload or a grounded Setra excitation). The B-coded gauge connectors J1–J4 cannot cross-mate with any of these. A true 4-terminal network: keep −Exc and −Out on separate conductors all the way to their buses. The Setra manual explicitly supports differential readout (its multi-hookup Option 1), which is how CH7 now reads it.

§4 · DIN-rail terminals

Terminal block schedule

Feed-through screw/spring blocks on Rail A (power) and Rail C (signal). Adjacent same-bus terminals are linked with push-in jumper bars — one incoming feed serves the whole bus.

Every bulkhead's pigtail lands on its own 5-way strip first (TB-J1–TB-J8, terminal numbers = M12 contacts), then internal panel wires fan out to the buses and the DAQ — so a student wires one connector at a time, and the shunt leads park on their own terminals. Termination count: 76 feed-through ways in use — landings TB-J1–J8 (8×5 = 40), GND bus G1–G6 (6 — spare bus blocks removed from the strip), V1 bus A1–A10 (10), V2 bus B1–B10 (10), iso pair X1–X2 (2), AC mains groups L1–L4 + N1–N4 (8) — plus 2 fuse terminals (FA, FB) and ~20% spares. Blocks are Phoenix UT 2,5 screw-clamp, color-coded by function (all order numbers verified on phoenixcontact.com) — RED 3045062 = +24 V, ORANGE 3045046 = +12 V, BLACK 3045088 = power grounds, BLUE 3044089 = AGND, YELLOW 3045059 = the GND_24ISO island (never bus yellow to anything), WHITE 3045075 = signals, GRAY 3044076 = the AC L/N groups — so block color = the wire color landing on it, and never use green/yellow-striped PE blocks (they bond to the rail). Ferrule every stranded conductor — screw clamps reward them. Quantities rounded up to the nearest 5 with spares: 115 blocks total (10 red, 15 orange, 25 black, 30 blue, 10 yellow, 15 white, 10 gray; 76 in service) + PT 4-HESI (5X20) fuse terminals (3211861, ×3), plus D-UT 2,5/10 end covers (3047028, ×20) and CLIPFIX 35 end brackets (3022218, ×20 — the landing groups sit spaced ~20 mm apart, two brackets per group). Insertion bridges (FBS family, confirmed numbers): FBS 5-5 3030190 ×5, FBS 10-5 3030213 ×2 (now spares), FBS 3-5 3030174 ×2, FBS 4-5 3030187 ×3 — the 6-block GND bus is bridged with one FBS 5-5 + one FBS 3-5 staggered; the landing strips are never bridged. Panel H/L jumper colors as built (HI/LO): white/blue = gauges + pressure, yellow/green = load cells.

§5 · Panel

Panel layout

  • Rail A / Rail C separation keeps supply wiring and signal wiring in different duct sections; run power down the left half of the duct, signals down the right.
  • The IAA100 amplifiers mount at the flume next to their load cells (splash hood or small enclosure — they are not submersible); each amp's 5-conductor cable W12–W14 runs to its panel connector J5–J7. Rail B stays as spare space.
  • The USB-1608G is a desktop-style module: mount with adhesive DIN clips or a small shelf bracket near the lower right so both screw-terminal banks (1–27 and 28–54) stay reachable.
  • End-clamps at both ends of every terminal group; marker tags on every terminal (S1…, A1…, G1…) matching the schedules in §4.

§6 · Grounding & shields

One ground point: DAQ analog ground

  • Single-point ground. Every power island references DAQ AGND exactly once, through the GND bus — gauges via their shields, the force rail via the A6 jumper, the Setra via −Out. Akamina calls AGND "the only ground reference in the system." In differential mode these references double as the DC return paths the 1608G manual requires for each channel.
  • Wave gauges: the black shield-substitute wire (gauge pin D) lands on the AGND bus at the panel end only — that is the gauge domain's reference. Signal low now runs to its CHxL terminal (blue wire), not to the bus. Power low is already bonded to the shield inside the gauge — do not ground it again at the supply, and do not add a ground at the probe (Akamina measured doubled noise doing so).
  • Load cells: shield floats at the cell by design; land it on the amp's chassis screw (per the current FUTEK manual), with a short bond from chassis to the output-ground terminal so the shield is referenced. One end only — done.
  • Setra 239: shield = case; bond to the panel earth stud for EMC (but never to −Exc — that bond is for grounded-readout topologies, not this one). −Exc stays on GND_24ISO (A3's floating return) and touches nothing else. −Out feeds CH7L and carries the island's single AGND tie.
  • Supplies float by construction. All three HDRs are Class II (no earth bond). Never strap any output to earth.
  • Panel earth. If the panel is metal, bond it to building earth via the mains inlet's PE and the stud; that earth serves the inlet PE, the Setra case/shield, and chassis safety only — never route signal returns through it.
  • Force rail (new for differential): one blue jumper from A6 (0V-A / GND_24) to the GND bus. The amps common their output and power grounds internally, so this single jumper references all three force channels; without it CH4–CH6 drift out of the common-mode window.

Open items to verify before wiring

  • BK supply model. Resolved twice over — it's a BK 1672, and the final design retires it to the bench anyway: the panel is self-powered by three Class II HDR supplies (2x HDR-15-24 + 1x HDR-15-12, all outputs floating). Use the BK for first power-up of subassemblies, where its current limit catches wiring faults.
  • Setra 239: which termination color code your unit has (match against the IF-E table), and its actual supply current from the datasheet label. Also check for a violet lead (Option 621/622 bipolar variant). Resolved: the unit's label reads 2391-015WB1F23259NN, ±15 inWC, excitation 22–30 VDC single supply, output ±2.5 VDC — the standard 4-wire unit. The isolated-island design stands; no bipolar rails needed.
  • Gauge cable lengths W1–W4 per flume station, plus service loop.
