Wave Flume DAQ - Sensor Interfaces, Cabling & Panel Layout

Eight analog sensors (4 wave-height gauges, 3 load cells, 1 differential pressure transducer) into one MCC USB-1608G - 16-bit, all eight channels differential - with signal conditioning, power distribution, and DIN-rail terminal wiring on a single mounting panel. Pinouts below are taken from the manufacturer manuals cited at the end.

§1 · System

Interface overview

Everything lands on DIN-rail terminal blocks first - sensors are field-replaceable without rewiring the DAQ. Every sensor detaches at a panel bulkhead connector, and every position is an M12 pair - a Phoenix push-lock plug on the field wires mating a TE receptacle whose 0.5 m pigtail runs into the box: wave gauges at J1–J4 (B-code), amplified load-cell lines at J5–J7 and pressure at J8 (A-code; the IAA100s live at the flume next to their cells). B-coded J1–J4 physically cannot mate the A-coded positions; within J5–J8 the connectors are identical, so label them. Only the bench supply leads and USB enter through glands.

§2 · Connector & interface pinouts

Every mating surface, pin by pin

IF-A - Wave gauge connector (gauge ↔ cable W1–W4)

Each AWP-24-3 has a 6-pin circular bayonet receptacle, Amphenol PT02A10-6P. The mating cable plug PT06E-10-6S (AWPC-01) ships with each gauge. As built, W1–W4 are 2-pair shielded instrumentation cable (Belden 8723 style - the construction Akamina recommends): red + black pair = power, green + white pair = signal, plus the shield drain.

IF-B - Panel bulkheads J1–J4 (cable ↔ panel, M12 B-code)

The panel end of each gauge cable W1–W4 gets a TE T4110402051-000 field-wireable M12 B-coded plug - unshielded plastic body, screw terminals, 18–24 AWG, PG9 gland (heat-shrink the twisted bundle where the gland clamps - it seals on a round cable, ~6–8 mm). It mates panel receptacle J1/J2/J3/J4, a TE T4171010405-002 (M12 B-code 5-pos male, rear-mount, 0.5 m pigtail) whose leads land on the DIN rails. B-coding is the keying: these four cannot be plugged into the A-coded J5–J8, and vice versa. The cable drain rides contact 5, so the shield grounds only at the GND bus (Akamina's single-point rule) - the plastic plug has no shell to terminate.

IF-C - Load cell ↔ amplifier (IAA100 sensor-side terminal, 4-pos)

The amplifiers live at the flume, next to their load cells (splash hood or small enclosure - they are not submersible). The LSB210's integral 5 ft cable (29 AWG, 4-conductor, spiral-shielded silicone) wires directly into the IAA100's 4-position sensor terminal block - the "cable entry housing, 4POS, sensor side" you already have. Fold or tin the fine 29 AWG leads so the terminals grip. Per the current FUTEK manual, the cable shield lands on the amp's CHASSIS screw (the enclosure body), not on pin 4 - add a short bond wire from the chassis screw to the output-ground terminal so the shield is referenced (one point only; the cell end floats). IF-D - Amplifier power & output through panel connectors J5–J7

Each amp's power/output block connects by a 5-conductor shielded cable (W12–W14, 20–24 AWG) to a TE T4110001051-000 A-code screw-terminal plug (unshielded, PG7 gland), mating panel receptacle J5/J6/J7 (TE T4171010005-002, 0.5 m pigtail into the box). The M12 contacts follow the panel's common as-built scheme (1 = power+, 2 = signal HI, 3 = signal LO, 4 = power−, 5 = shunt), so the amp pins cross over inside the cable - conductors as built: red +VIN, white VOUT, blue OUT_GND, black PWR_GND, orange SHUNT:

DIP switches: SW1 excitation at 10 V (factory default) with the default 2 mV/V gain range gives ±10 V full scale - an exact match to the DAQ's ±10 V range. IF-E - Pressure transducer flying leads (Setra 239)

The 239 has a captive cable with one of two factory color codes - check yours against both columns. It plugs in at panel connector J8 - the same TE T4110001051-000 plug + TE T4171010005-002 receptacle pair as J5–J7 (contacts as built: 1 = +Exc, 2 = +Out, 3 = −Out, 4 = −Exc, 5 = shield/case - the same 1 PWR+ / 2 HI / 3 LO / 4 PWR− scheme as every other bulkhead). Because J8 is physically identical to the amp jacks J5–J7, label all four unmistakably - cross-plugging misroutes the 24 V domains (iso-module overload or a grounded Setra excitation). The B-coded gauge connectors J1–J4 cannot cross-mate with any of these. A true 4-terminal network: keep −Exc and −Out on separate conductors all the way to their buses. The Setra manual explicitly supports differential readout (its multi-hookup Option 1), which is how CH7 now reads it.

As built, five supplies sit on the middle rail row: the three active ones above plus a mounted 12 V spare and 24 V spare beside them (AC-fed, outputs unwired - swap wires over if a unit dies). The DC-side fuse labels on the panel read 12 V: 0.5 A, 24 V: 0.25 A, 24 V-ISO: 0.2 A - note this differs from the FA/FB plan in §7 (verify against the installed cartridges). Each rail still ends up referenced to DAQ analog ground exactly once: the 24 V rail through the blue A6 → GND-bus jumper (added for differential mode - the amps' OutGnds are now CH4L–CH6L inputs), the 12 V rail through the gauges' internal power-low-to-shield bond, and A3's output only through the Setra's −Out. One IEC C14 inlet (switched + fused, wall-mounted - a standard C13 computer cord plugs in) feeds all three supplies through jumpered mains-rated L/N terminal groups on the rail; being Class II they take no earth conductor - the panel earth stud still serves only the Setra case.

§4 · DIN-rail terminals

Terminal block schedule

Feed-through screw blocks across the three as-built rail rows (§5): landings + AC entry on TB ROW 1, buses + supplies on TB ROW 2, DAQ on TB ROW 3. Adjacent same-bus terminals are linked with push-in jumper bars - one incoming feed serves the whole bus.

Every bulkhead's pigtail lands on its own 5-way strip first (TB-J1–TB-J8, terminal numbers = M12 contacts), then internal panel wires fan out to the buses and the DAQ - so the installer wires one connector at a time, and the shunt leads park on their own terminals. Termination count: 76 feed-through ways in use - landings TB-J1–J8 (8×5 = 40), GND bus G1–G6 (6 - spare bus blocks removed from the strip), V1 bus A1–A10 (10), V2 bus B1–B10 (10), iso pair X1–X2 (2), AC mains groups L1–L4 + N1–N4 (8) - plus 2 fuse terminals (FA, FB) and ~20% spares. Blocks are Phoenix UT 2,5 screw-clamp, color-coded by function (all order numbers verified on phoenixcontact.com) - RED 3045062 = +24 V, YELLOW 3045059 = +12 V, BLACK 3045088 = power grounds, BLUE 3044089 = AGND, ORANGE 3045046 = the GND_24ISO island (never bus orange to anything), WHITE 3045075 = signals, GRAY 3044076 = the AC L/N groups. (As built the installer swapped the planned orange/yellow roles: yellow = 12 V, orange = Setra iso island - wire colors follow the same scheme) - so block color = the wire color landing on it, and never use green/yellow-striped PE blocks (they bond to the rail). Ferrule every stranded conductor - screw clamps reward them. Quantities rounded up to the nearest 5 with spares: 115 blocks total (10 red, 15 orange, 25 black, 30 blue, 10 yellow, 15 white, 10 gray; 76 in service) + PT 4-HESI (5X20) fuse terminals (3211861, ×3), plus D-UT 2,5/10 end covers (3047028, ×20) and CLIPFIX 35 end brackets (3022218, ×20 - the landing groups sit spaced ~20 mm apart, two brackets per group). Insertion bridges (FBS family, confirmed numbers): FBS 5-5 3030190 ×5, FBS 10-5 3030213 ×2 (now spares), FBS 3-5 3030174 ×2, FBS 4-5 3030187 ×3 - the 6-block GND bus is bridged with one FBS 5-5 + one FBS 3-5 staggered; the landing strips are never bridged. Panel H/L jumper colors as built (HI/LO): white/blue = gauges + pressure, yellow/green = load cells.

§5 · Panel

Panel layout

  • Gland plate labels use the sensor names (WG1…PT1); this document's J1–J8 designators map to them in order - J1–J4 = WG1–WG4, J5–J7 = LC1–LC3, J8 = PT1.
  • The IAA100 amplifiers mount at the flume next to their load cells (splash hood or small enclosure - they are not submersible); each amp's 5-conductor cable W12–W14 runs to its panel connector.
  • Signal pairs stay twisted from strip to DAQ; power and signal run in separate duct sections.
  • End-clamps at both ends of every terminal group; the printed strip legends list each terminal's wire color and function (fix the LC "5-GRY-SHLD" legends - term 5 is the SHUNT).

§6 · Grounding & shields

One ground point: DAQ analog ground

  • Single-point ground. Every power island references DAQ AGND exactly once, through the GND bus - gauges via their shields, the force rail via the A6 jumper, the Setra via −Out. Akamina calls AGND "the only ground reference in the system." In differential mode these references double as the DC return paths the 1608G manual requires for each channel.
  • Wave gauges: the cable drain (gauge pin D) lands on the AGND bus at the panel end only - that is the gauge domain's reference. Signal low now runs to its CHxL terminal (blue wire), not to the bus. Power low is already bonded to the shield inside the gauge - do not ground it again at the supply, and do not add a ground at the probe (Akamina measured doubled noise doing so).
  • Load cells: shield floats at the cell by design; land it on the amp's chassis screw (per the current FUTEK manual), with a short bond from chassis to the output-ground terminal so the shield is referenced. One end only - done.
  • Setra 239: shield = case; bond to the panel earth stud for EMC (but never to −Exc - that bond is for grounded-readout topologies, not this one). −Exc stays on GND_24ISO (A3's floating return) and touches nothing else. −Out feeds CH7L and carries the island's single AGND tie.
  • Supplies float by construction. All three HDRs are Class II (no earth bond). Never strap any output to earth.
  • Panel earth. If the panel is metal, bond it to building earth via the mains inlet's PE and the stud; that earth serves the inlet PE, the Setra case/shield, and chassis safety only - never route signal returns through it.
  • Force rail (new for differential): one blue jumper from A6 (0V-A / GND_24) to the GND bus. The amps common their output and power grounds internally, so this single jumper references all three force channels; without it CH4–CH6 drift out of the common-mode window.

Open items to verify before wiring

  • BK supply model. Resolved twice over - it's a BK 1672, and the final design retires it to the bench anyway: the panel is self-powered by three Class II HDR supplies (2x HDR-15-24 + 1x HDR-15-12, all outputs floating). Use the BK for first power-up of subassemblies, where its current limit catches wiring faults.
  • Setra 239: which termination color code your unit has (match against the IF-E table), and its actual supply current from the datasheet label. Also check for a violet lead (Option 621/622 bipolar variant). Resolved: the unit's label reads 2391-015WB1F23259NN, ±15 inWC, excitation 22–30 VDC single supply, output ±2.5 VDC - the standard 4-wire unit. The isolated-island design stands; no bipolar rails needed.
  • Gauge cable lengths W1–W4 per flume station, plus service loop.
  • IAA100 DIN-mount accessory availability - otherwise screw-mount the housings.
  • After first power-up: set InstaCal to DIFFERENTIAL and the per-channel ranges (CH7 ±5 V, all others ±10 V), allow the 15-minute warm-up, then shunt-cal each IAA100 (pin 3) and record gauge calibration slopes (V per cm of immersion) into the acquisition software.
  • Relabel LC strips: the printed "5-GRY-SHLD" legend on LC1–LC3 is wrong - contact 5 carries the IAA100 SHUNT line (orange at the amp, FI1573). LC shields stay at the amp chassis screw.
  • DC fuse ratings as installed read 12 V: 0.5 A / 24 V: 0.25 A / 24 V-ISO: 0.2 A - differs from the FA (24 V, 0.5 A time-delay) / FB (12 V, 0.25 A) plan in §7. Confirm cartridges ride out the 3× IAA100 inrush on the 24 V rail.
  • Noise debug (2026-10, one gauge connected): first re-land the wires per the 1608G terminal numbers - the old single-ended map put them on the wrong physical screws - then repeat with the laptop on battery to rule out a USB ground loop.
